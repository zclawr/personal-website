About

I am a first-year PhD student in Computer Science at the University of Wisconsin–Madison, where I work on machine learning for scientific computing. I am broadly interested in AI for science, the foundations of machine learning, scientific computing, and time series analysis: in particular, how learned models can stand in for expensive simulations without giving up the reliability that scientists need from them.

I completed my B.S. in Computer Science at UC San Diego in 2026, with minors in Physics and Mathematics. As an undergraduate I worked in Rose Yu's Spatiotemporal Lab on Bayesian active learning for deep surrogate models of turbulent transport in nuclear fusion simulations, in collaboration with General Atomics, and built the Kubernetes-based job scheduling used to run those simulations on the NSF Nautilus cluster. In an NSF REU at Washington State University Vancouver, I developed deep learning models for forecasting and dispatching power from hybrid wind farms with integrated energy storage. With Engineers for Exploration I led the Mangrove Monitoring team, which built a web platform for identifying mangroves in aerial imagery with semantic segmentation, and later trained lightweight bird-call classifiers for deployment on embedded devices. I also worked with Massimiliano Di Ventra's group on using dynamical systems methods to understand LSTM training dynamics.
